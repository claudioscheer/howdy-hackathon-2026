The homepage is empty. The product is not.

The home page has one job: present what Howdy is, then send the engineering manager to Login. Not trial limits, not expiration, not shareable links. Those live inside the product. Right now the screen is a giant name on an empty grid. These five first viewports keep Login as the only door, and put the interviewer on stage.

Costume telemetry

Coordinates, a signal meter, and “2-trial expiring seams” sit in the corners with nothing to measure. Chrome without a mission reads as theme. Policy details do not belong in the lobby.

Name, not behavior

“Howdy Interview Coach” is the hero. A first-time visitor cannot tell that this interviewer presses when an answer is weak, and that practice actually feels like the real thing.

The door is fine

Login is the right next step. Keep it. The miss is everything around it: empty grid, operational labels, no picture of the interview itself.

01 Cold open

The interviewer speaks first. Login is the only door.

Do this if
You want someone to feel the interviewer in one breath. The page asks a real question. Login is still the only action.
What changes
Product name drops to a quiet mark. The question is the display type. No answer field, no attempt counter. One ghost Login.
Risk
It can feel like a demo that never starts. The question has to be specific enough that people recognize a real interview.

05 The question as architecture

Keep the film-title composition. Change the title from the product name to the thing the product actually says.
